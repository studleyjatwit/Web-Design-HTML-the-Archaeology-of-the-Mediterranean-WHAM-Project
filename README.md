# Web-Design-HTML-the-Archaeology-of-the-Mediterranean-WHAM-Project

Website project for CSAS Studio at Wentworth Institute of Technology 

# The Team 
Jamison Studley, Jeremie Hodgedon, Eouin Darlington

# Purpose
The WHAM project’s goal is to bring together your front-end HTML
programming skills, your views on web design and CSS layout, the programs we have used
in labs (e.g., ThingLink, MyMaps, TimeLineJS etc.), along with your new knowledge of
archaeology to create a multicomponent and multimedia website that presents the
archaeology of a Mediterranean region to the public. 

This semester we are focusing on the provinces of the Roman Empire, as they present some incredible archaeological
remains and some well-recorded history. Moreover, your professor is a Roman archaeology
specialist, so he can provide insights on nearly every major region of the empire.

# Learning Outcomes
Students should be able to:

Conduct interdisciplinary research into the archaeological history of a region
and into best practices for digitally expressing archaeological research to the
public.

Apply appropriate digital methods of inquiry into these issues, including the use
of generative AI, and to evaluate and communicate the results of these
applications.

# Project Must Haves 
Part A: Archaeology and HTML 
1. Historical analysis of the region
2. Analysis of regional geography and resources
3. Introduction to major regional sites and key cultural aspects (e.g., languages, religion,
political system, main economic base, and technological achievements)
4. Introduction to major regional artifact types (ceramics, metals, statues, coins, animal bones, 
etc.)
5. Detailed study of a famous building
6. Detailed study of a famous artifact
7. Voiceover introduction to a famous historical personality (using ThingLink).
8. Discussion of ethical issues related to regional archaeology
9. Recommendations for visitors to the region; suggestions of other websites; OR ideas for a
video game concept.
10. Works cited and reflexive AI literacy and tool use overview.

Part B: Web Design and HTML
1. Effective introductory homepage (index.html)
2. CSS: Effective color, font, and intuitive structural layouts for all pages
3. HTML: Effective use of images, video, and sound
4. Use of a JavaScript Button(s) or other programmable aspect to execute an action
5. Some use of data visualization using data from www.opencontext.org or Mantis
(https://numismatics.org/search/) that can be displayed in Tableau or StoryLine JS.
6. Use of Google MyMaps with data points and layers to show archaeological information
7. Use of ThingLink
8. Use of Timeline JS
9. Use of an organized file folder and file path structure.
10. Final presentation/explication of code and AI usage
